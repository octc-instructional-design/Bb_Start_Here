Ask for the Help You Actually Need

A good prompt tells AI where you’re stuck, what kind of help would move you forward, and what you want to do yourself.

← AI Literacy

Check the assignment first. Use these examples only when your instructor allows that kind of help. If you’re unsure, check what to do before you ask.

Give your prompt three parts

  1. What I’m working on: Name the topic or task.
  2. Where I’m stuck: Say what you’ve tried or what isn’t clear.
  3. What help I want: Ask for a hint, a question, an example, or feedback. Say what you want to do yourself.
“I’m working on ____. I’m stuck on ____. Help me by ____. Leave ____ for me to do.”

Try a prompt that fits your task

Study a concept

“I’m learning about opportunity cost. I understand the definition, but I have trouble spotting it in an example. Give me one everyday scenario and ask me what the opportunity cost is. Wait for my answer before explaining.”

Brainstorm a topic

“I’m choosing a topic for a short presentation. I have two ideas, but I’m not sure which I can cover in five minutes. Ask me three questions to help me decide. Leave the choice and outline to me.”

Get feedback on your draft

“I wrote this paragraph and want to know if my reasoning is clear. Point to one place a reader might get confused and ask me a question about it. I’ll revise it myself.”

If the answer goes too far, ask again

Try: “That gave me the whole answer. Back up and give me just one hint, then let me try.” If AI misses the point, say what it misunderstood and ask for one specific change.

Keep private information and restricted course materials out of prompts. Before relying on an answer, check it against your course materials or an original source.
