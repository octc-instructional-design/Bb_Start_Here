Understand Your Grades

Start with the syllabus. Courses may calculate grades differently. The number of assignments in a category does not necessarily tell you how much that category affects your final grade.

Points-based grading

Your grade is generally the points you earned divided by the points possible, multiplied by 100. Higher-point assignments have more influence.

Example: Earning 450 out of 500 possible points gives you 90%. A 100-point project counts as much as ten 10-point activities.

Weighted grading

Each category contributes a set percentage of your grade. Your category average is multiplied by that category’s weight, then the results are added together.

Example: If exams are 50%, assignments are 30%, and discussions are 20%, category averages of 80%, 90%, and 100% produce:

(80 × 0.50) + (90 × 0.30) + (100 × 0.20) = 87%

In this example, exams count for half of your final grade, even if you complete many more discussions. How individual items count within each category depends on the course’s setup.

Read your current grade carefully

  • A current grade may reflect only work graded so far. Ungraded work may not yet affect it; a missing assignment entered as zero does.
  • Check the syllabus for category weights, dropped scores, extra credit, late-work rules, and the letter-grade scale.
  • Do not simply average the percentages shown on individual assignments unless that matches the course’s grading method.
  • If the calculation is unclear, ask your instructor how the current grade is calculated and which work is included.
