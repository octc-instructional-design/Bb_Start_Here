Email Your Instructor

You do not need fancy language. A clear, courteous message helps your instructor understand what you need.

  1. Use the right channel. Check the syllabus for whether your instructor prefers email or Blackboard messages. When emailing, use your college email account.
  2. Write a useful subject. Include the course, section, and topic—for example, “ENG 101, Section [your section] — Question about Essay 1.”
  3. Greet your instructor and identify yourself. Use the name or title they have asked you to use. Include your full name and course section.
  4. Be specific. Name the assignment, explain what you already checked or tried, and ask a clear question. If a deadline is involved, include it.
  5. Review before sending. Use complete sentences, check your tone and attachments, and close with your name. Keep passwords and sensitive personal information out of your message.

An email you can adapt

Replace the bracketed information with your own details.

Subject: ENG 101, Section [your section] — Question about Essay 1 sources

Hello Professor [Last name],

I’m [Full name] in your ENG 101 [section] class. I read the Essay 1 directions and the rubric, but I’m unsure whether our textbook counts toward the two required sources. Could you clarify?

The essay is due [date], and I’d like to make sure I’m choosing appropriate sources before I finish my draft.

Thank you,
[Full name]

Check your instructor’s stated response time before following up. If that time has passed, reply in the same email thread with a brief, courteous reminder. Keep working on what you can while you wait.
