Take two minutes to see what’s draining your energy and choose one small next step. Try this check-in at the end of the day, at the start of the week, or whenever you feel stretched.

1. Check your energy batteries

You can have time to do something and still feel like you don’t have the energy for it. The five batteries help you name what kind of energy is running low and decide what might help.

Physical Mental Emotional Social Spiritual

Try a daily or weekly check-in: Look at each battery and ask, “Does it feel charged, low, or nearly empty?” Pick one to pay attention to. What’s draining it? What would help you recharge?

  • Physical

    Your body’s energy for getting through the day.

    What can drain it: Too little sleep, skipping meals, or sitting for hours.

    What might recharge it: Eat something, take a short walk, or give yourself time to rest.

  • Mental

    Your energy for focusing, making decisions, and solving problems.

    What can drain it: Switching between tasks, too many tabs, or trying to remember everything.

    What might recharge it: Write down what’s on your mind, then work on one task for a short stretch.

  • Emotional

    Your energy for handling feelings, stress, and conflict.

    What can drain it: A hard conversation, worry, or carrying a lot without talking about it.

    What might recharge it: Name what you’re feeling or talk it through with someone you trust.

  • Social

    Your energy for connecting with other people.

    What can drain it: Too much time alone, or too many social demands without a break.

    What might recharge it: Text someone you miss or give yourself some quiet time. Notice which helps.

  • Spiritual

    Your sense of meaning, purpose, and what matters to you. This can include faith, but doesn’t have to.

    What can drain it: Going through the motions or losing sight of why you’re doing something.

    What might recharge it: Think about why you started college, or make time for something you value.

2. Choose one small next step

Try this: “My ____ battery is running low. One thing I can do to recharge it is ____.”

That might mean taking a real break, texting someone you trust, or asking for help with one thing that feels heavy.

Make your goal doable

  • Pick one action you can control. “Take a 10-minute walk” gives you somewhere to start. “Be less stressed” doesn’t tell you what to do next.
  • Give it a time or cue. “After my last class today, I’ll walk for 10 minutes before I head home.”
  • Make a smaller backup plan. If 10 minutes won’t fit, take a two-minute stretch break. Then ask: Did it help? What would I change next time?
